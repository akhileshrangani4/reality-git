# Native splat renderer notes

Verified September 8, 2026: the iPhone 15 Pro build now renders captured depth points using iOS 27's native `GaussianSplatComponent` and `GaussianSplatResource`. The user confirmed seeing the remembered shape on the phone. This validates the preview, not movement, restoration, or measured anchor accuracy.

The MVP freezes a first-view LiDAR surface with sampled camera colors, with up to 4,000 points per observation. Red retains this immutable surface at its original world position, with 6 mm splat scales and 25% per-splat opacity. Green uses the latest measured surface and its matching world position, with 3 mm scales and 4.5% per-splat opacity. Overlapping splats still accumulate opacity. These are partial visible surfaces, not optimized multi-view reconstructions. The original cloud and world position survive tracking loss and reselection; “Start over” clears them. The preview toggle displays the saved red shape independently of the automatic diff.

Astra supplies identity and the object outline. Vision advances that outline between replies; calibrated depth produces the measured surface. When local advancement is unavailable, the UI can display Astra's last observed world surface within its existing authority lifetime, labeled “last seen.” This is historical evidence and may lag a moving object.

Green no longer translates the original reference cloud to a new median position. Its point offsets, bounds, center, and timestamp travel together from the same measurement. One native buffer is reused across observations; unused slots become transparent when the point count decreases. A fixed world-origin anchor contains the surface entity, whose position is the observation center. Buffer updates occur once per new visible observation, rather than once per AR display frame.

When current 3D geometry is missing after a confirmed change, the app can show a green rectangle on a fresh trusted local image track, with 6% fill opacity and a softened border. That fallback does not supply a world position or alter diff state, and it expires with tracking freshness. Native rendering failures use the current observation's bounds for green's translucent proxy.

Apple's [component documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/gaussiansplatcomponent) supplies a buffer-based example and specifies Apple7 GPU-family support. The renderer expects positions, scale, quaternion rotation, opacity and spherical-harmonic coefficients; the app must parse its asset format. Quaternion buffer order is scalar first (`r, x, y, z`), so do not copy SIMD quaternion storage blindly.

Use explicit offsets and strides, and align `LowLevelBuffer` capacity as required. Resource creation can throw for invalid descriptors or excessive splat counts. Keep the proxy on failure. The renderer blends transparent splats and does not require an app-authored shader. Uniform ghost tint must be applied to appearance data rather than a mesh material. [GaussianSplatResource](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/gaussiansplatresource) exposes scale/opacity activation controls; match those to the asset's representation to avoid applying exponentials or sigmoid twice.

The current buffer uses scalar-first identity quaternions, explicit metric scales, identity scale/opacity activations, and degree-zero spherical-harmonic colors. Phone logs previously confirmed native resources with 846 and 1,536 captured points. The green alignment correction passes a signed device build and all 52 core tests and is installed on the iPhone. Quantitative alignment, opacity, depth interaction, larger budgets, and the automatic move/remove/restore cycle still need physical validation.

Capture feedback uses a scattered, stateful particle spray in the same white tint as the camera buttons. Buttons, loading indicator, particle heads and trails share `AppPalette.controlRGB`. A randomized aperture, launch cone and speed spread open up the stream. Three scales of smooth curl flow bend individual trajectories while bounded velocity steering gathers them near the measured target. Particles retain their world-space momentum when the camera moves. Slower flight and turbulence evolution make travel readable. The stream fades over its final 5.5 cm and particles are retired by swept contact against a 9 mm region. There is no flash, glow or outward burst at the contact point. This is an artistic feedback effect, not a fluid solver or mesh collision simulation.

The flow follows the vector-potential approach in [Bridson, Hourihan and Nordenstam (2007)](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~rbridson/docs/bridson-siggraph2007-curlnoise.pdf), using inexpensive analytic trigonometric modes instead of Perlin noise. The curl field itself is divergence-free; the added target attraction intentionally converges. Velocity steering and the slowdown region are adapted from [Reynolds (1999)](https://www.red3d.com/cwr/steer/gdc99/). Simulation advances at fixed 1/120-second steps, caps catch-up work and clears stale particles after long pauses. A pool caps flight particles at 168; heads and trails use at most 336 splats in one reusable 352-slot native buffer. Stopping emission fades the entire effect out within 0.32 seconds, without waiting for the slower particles to finish their journey.

The buffer is created with populated geometry and placed relative to its impact point. The selected depth point only aims this effect; it never establishes identity or reference geometry. Once captured, the spray settles onto a measured surface point nearest the reference center. Emission follows initial Astra work and the surface reveal, then drains. Reset, pause, unreliable AR, and Reduce Motion stop the effect. Red points form outward from the impact over 0.42 seconds with a fixed stagger, using a smaller scale and lower opacity for the temporary capture preview. Normal red diff appearance is restored when needed. Rendering does not wait for the animation.

The Debug-only `--capture-preview` launch argument runs a deterministic non-AR rendering fixture without network calls. Add `--capture-reduce-motion` to inspect the static alternative. It saves four `capture-preview-*.png` snapshots in the app's Documents directory for scanning, partial reveal, completed capture, and a moved-object diff. Both modes were rendered on the iPhone 15 Pro and their screenshots inspected: particles converge on the fixture, the red surface reveals progressively, temporary effects clear, and Reduce Motion suppresses the effects. Normal camera flow was restored afterward and captured a native 3,717-point surface. Signed Debug build and all 52 core tests pass; this visual check does not measure sustained frame rate or real-world alignment.

The September 9 scattered-spray revision passes the signed device build and all 59 core tests. Seven simulation tests cover refresh-rate consistency, spread and convergence, slower arrival and draining, camera movement, invalid input and pause cleanup, moving-target bounds, and the curl field's numerical divergence.

The final white spray was rendered on the iPhone 15 Pro: snapshots confirm the button-matched color, clear contact point, subtle red reveal and complete effect cleanup. Reduce Motion was also checked on the slower simulation and suppresses the particles while preserving the red diff. A 2.3-second Debug fixture sample recorded 56.6 display callbacks/s and a 5.54 ms p95 CPU update cost for scan effects; this includes snapshot overhead and is not a sustained GPU frame-rate or live-AR measurement. The normal camera app was restored after inspection.
